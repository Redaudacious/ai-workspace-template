# Documentation for <project name>

The documentation follows the **Divio model**, also known as Diátaxis: four kinds of
text, each serving a distinct purpose. A single document that tries to cover all four
ends up doing none of them well.

The map below shows where to look, depending on what is needed.

| If… | Then | Where |
|---|---|---|
| this is the first encounter with the project and learning it step by step is the goal | **Tutorial** | [tutorial/](tutorial/) |
| there is a concrete goal and the steps to reach it are needed | **How-to guides** | [how-to/](how-to/) |
| a value, a path, or a setting name is needed | **Reference** | [reference/](reference/) |
| the reason something is done a certain way is needed | **Explanation** | [explanation/](explanation/) |

## The difference between them, in brief

**The tutorial** is a lesson. It takes someone who knows nothing and guides them,
step by step, to a visible result. It offers no options and does not explain
alternatives — those would confuse. It must work every time.

**The how-to guides** are recipes. They assume the reader already knows the domain and
answer a question that only someone with experience could formulate. They do not
explain concepts; they point to explanations through links.

**The reference** describes the mechanism. It is dry, exact, and complete. It teaches
nobody anything and does not say why — it says only what exists and what values it takes.

**The explanations** discuss. Here is the place for reasons, rejected alternatives,
pitfalls, and context. No instructions are given.

The fifth kind, the **record** (a series of dated entries, appended to and never
rewritten), does not live here but in `records/`: the pitfalls, the journal, the
decisions, and the work items. A document here is rewritten when the thing it describes
changes; a record entry stays as it was written.
